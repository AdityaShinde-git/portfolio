# Signal Ridge — World Bible

Oct 25, 2026 · @tt

## 1. Vision and tone

Signal Ridge is a dusk-lit mountain country where six beacon fires have gone dark, and the visitor drives, walks and climbs to relight them. Each fire is one chapter of your portfolio: a skill area, its projects and its story.

**Premise.** Long ago the ridge was a chain of signal stations that passed light from the lowlands to the peak. The network failed one station at a time. You are the last runner. Relighting the stations restores the chain, and the final fire at The Last Signal sends your message out (the Contact moment).

**Mood.** Quiet, warm and a little lonely. Cold green-black land under a bruised red-pink sky, with small amber lights as the only warm colour. Nothing is hostile. The tension is distance and darkness, never combat.

**Three pillars**

- **Discovery over instruction.** The world explains itself through landmarks, field notes and light. No tutorial walls.
- **Light as progress.** Every restored signal visibly changes the world: warmer sky, lit windows, glowing roads, more birds.
- **Portfolio inside the place.** Content lives in the architecture of each location (a board at the trailhead, a bridge for frontend, a lantern village for backend), so the metaphor does the explaining.

**What the visitor should feel.** Curious in the first minute, calm during travel, rewarded at each fire, and a little sorry to leave. A full visit should take 8 to 15 minutes, with a quick path for recruiters who only want the Work, Skills and Contact links in the top bar.

## 2. The world at a glance

The world is one continuous valley-and-ridge landscape of about 6 km by 3.8 km, laid out so the visitor travels west to east, from low green country up into bare high ridges.

**Scale.** The map is 1000 by 640 units at 6 m per unit. On foot (5 km/h) the full width takes about 70 minutes, which is too long, so the default way to travel is the vehicle (about 60 km/h, roughly 6 minutes across) plus fast travel from the map.

**Shape.** A river runs north to south through the west-centre. A high range fills the north-east. A rugged pass and quarry country fills the south-east. The settlement sits in the middle valley where roads meet.

**The journey.** The six signals follow the story order Languages, Frontend, Backend, Databases, Architecture, Tools. The route climbs from the Trailhead in the west, crosses the river, passes through the middle valley, then splits: one road to the Old Giant and the final peak, one to the High Pass.

| Region | Position | Elevation feel | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Lowlands | West | Gentle, open meadow | Trailhead, start area |
| Riverlands | North-west | Wet valley, forest | River Crossing, waterfall |
| The Ashen Vale | Centre | Flat valley floor | Ashen Settlement, crossroads |
| Fossil Ridge | East-centre | Rocky spine | The Old Giant, Mirror Tarn, fossil beds |
| Signal Range | North-east | Highest, bare peaks | The Last Signal |
| The High Pass | South-centre | Steep switchbacks | High Pass, quarry road |
| Motor Yard | South-west | Flat gravel yard | Motor Works workshop |

Location coordinates here are my placement on the new map and can be moved freely.

## 3. Regions and biomes

Each region needs its own silhouette, ground colour and sound so the visitor always knows where they are, even with the HUD hidden.

| Region | Terrain | Vegetation | Ground and palette | Weather bias |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Lowlands | Rolling meadow, low hills | Tall grass, scattered birch, fence lines | Olive green, dry gold grass | Clear, light wind |
| Riverlands | Cut valley, wet banks, waterfall | Dense pine, ferns, reeds, moss | Deep green, blue-grey water | Mist at dawn and dusk |
| The Ashen Vale | Wide flat basin | Sparse burnt trees, ash-grey scrub | Charcoal soil, grey-brown dust | Haze, drifting ash flecks |
| Fossil Ridge | Rock spine, exposed strata | Lichen, hardy grass, few trees | Sandstone tan, rust bands | Dry, strong wind |
| Signal Range | Steep peaks, scree, snow patches | Almost none above the tree line | Slate, pale grey, white | Cold, cloud banks, clearest sky at night |
| The High Pass | Switchbacks, cliff edges, quarry | Wind-bent pines, rock | Dark stone, orange dust | Gusts, low cloud |
| Motor Yard | Flat gravel lot, old sheds | Weeds through cracks | Oil-dark gravel, rust | Neutral |

**Between regions.** Blend ground colour over about 150 m rather than cutting hard lines. Use the river, a ridge or a tree line as the visual border wherever possible.

**Terrain rules**

- Keep the main road readable from 300 m in every region.
- No dead ends without a reason: each side valley holds a small landmark (see the landmark list below).
- Vertical drama goes in the east. The west should feel safe and open, so the first minute is calm.
- Use fog and distance haze to hide the world edge. The edge is a mountain wall, a cliff or the river gorge, never an invisible wall.

**Landmarks outside the six main locations**

- **Waterfall** (Riverlands): audible before it is visible, a good first discovery.
- **Old timber bridge** (west bank): collapsed in the middle, driveable by ford only.
- **Mirror Tarn** (Fossil Ridge): a still lake that reflects the sky, best photo spot.
- **Fossil beds**: ribs and a skull half out of the rock, which also explains the name of the Old Giant.
- **Watchtower ruin** (Lowlands): an earlier, broken signal station that hints at the story.
- **Quarry** (High Pass): working machinery shapes, a place for the vehicle to show off.
- **Fire ring** (Ashen Vale): a small camp that acts as a rest and save spot.

## 4. The six signals and Motor Works

Every location follows the same recipe: an approach that shows its silhouette from afar, a central structure the visitor can walk into, a content surface (board, sign, lantern, screen), and a signal fire that lights when the visitor interacts. Content named below is from your existing map and cards.

### 01 The Trailhead (Languages)

- **Look.** A wooden gate and a trail map on the edge of a meadow. A small hut, a bench and a long board on a post holding the complete skills inventory.
- **Role.** The start and the home base. Always open. The Skills Board lives here.
- **Interaction.** Walk to the board and press E to open the full skills view. Lighting it sends the first light down the road to the River Crossing.
- **Details.** Hand-written chalk notes, a thermos and a map pinned under a stone. The first birds fly over here.

### 02 The River Crossing (Frontend)

- **Look.** A wide ford with a timber bridge beside it, a waterfall upstream and a small hut with a wheel.
- **Role.** Frontend: React.js, TypeScript, JavaScript, HTML, and the work at V.K. Lalco and Volody.
- **Interaction.** Drive through the ford for splash and sound, or take the bridge. A signal post on the far bank opens the frontend card and the See Projects link.
- **Details.** The bridge planks glow gold when lit. Water reflects the sky and the fire.

### 03 The Ashen Settlement (Backend)

- **Look.** A loop of ten to twelve small houses around a lantern-lit road, ash drifting, one tall chimney.
- **Role.** Backend. The social hub and the crossroads of the world.
- **Interaction.** Each house is a backend topic. Stop at its porch and press E to reveal the topic and light its lamp; light all ten before the central signal can be relit.
- **Details.** Windows turn from dark to warm as signals are restored. A communal fire, benches and neighbours walking the village loop make the settlement feel inhabited; a horn gets a friendly wave.

### 04 The Old Giant (Databases)

- **Look.** A huge fossil skeleton frozen into a rock ridge with a vista point at its rib cage.
- **Role.** Databases: deep, old and structured.
- **Interaction.** Climb the path to the rib cage. The signal fire sits in the skull. A short lookout cinematic pans over the whole valley.
- **Details.** Layered strata along the cliff as a visual metaphor for data layers. The best long-view photo spot.

### 05 The Last Signal (Architecture)

- **Look.** The highest point: a stone beacon tower on the north-east peak, the tallest silhouette in the world, visible from nearly everywhere.
- **Role.** Architecture and the finale. It unlocks only when the first four are lit, and lighting it plays the ending and opens Contact.
- **Interaction.** A steep final climb. At the top, the beacon sends a beam across all six locations.
- **Details.** Wind, cloud below you, the whole map lit up in amber.

### 06 The High Pass (Tools)

- **Look.** Mountain switchbacks with a rusted gate, a lookout shed and a view down to the Ashen Vale.
- **Role.** Tools: the things that get you over the mountain.
- **Interaction.** A driving challenge section. The signal is at the top of the final switchback. Optional rally route (see section 7) starts here.
- **Details.** Tool-shaped props: wrenches, rope, a winch, carabiners on a rack.

### Motor Works (vehicle workshop)

- **Look.** A large corrugated workshop in a gravel yard at the south-west corner, with a lift, lights and a paint wall.
- **Role.** Not a signal. The place to customise the vehicle and enter the vehicle hub.
- **Interaction.** Drive in to open the workshop screen: colours, wheels, lights, horn. The Motor Works map entry can also travel directly to the workshop and return the vehicle to its prior location when leaving.
- **Details.** Radio playing, oil stains, tools on a pegboard, a lit sign you can see from the Lowlands.

**Order and gates.** Locations 1 to 4 can be visited in any order. Location 5 needs the first four, and 6 is open from the start but is the hardest drive. The Motor Works is always open.

## 5. Roads, river and travel

Roads are the world's skeleton. The map shows the same network the player drives, so every line on the map must exist in 3D.

| Road | Connects | Type | Surface and feel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Old Pass A | Trailhead to River Crossing | Main road | Worn gravel with a gentle curve, the safe first drive |
| Old Pass B | River Crossing to Ashen Settlement | Main road | Packed dirt through forest, two bends |
| Old Pass C | Ashen Settlement to Old Giant | Main road | Open and fast, long straights over the vale |
| Old Pass D | Old Giant to Last Signal | Main road | Climbs steadily, tighter with each bend |
| Old Pass E | Ashen Settlement to High Pass | Main road | Descends then climbs, stony |
| Switchback trail | High Pass to Old Giant | Trail | Narrow, loose, steep, a shortcut for brave drivers |
| Yard trail | Trailhead to Motor Works | Trail | Grass and ruts through the lowlands |
| Workshop trail | Motor Works to Ashen Settlement | Trail | Long diagonal, passes the old bridge |

**Road state.** A road is dim until both its ends are lit, then it glows amber on the map and its roadside posts light up in the world. This is the clearest way to show progress.

**Road and vehicle alignment.** The rendered track, terrain shaping, bridge deck and vehicle grounding share the graded spline surface. Main-road grades are limited to roughly 9.5%; the vehicle gets higher grip and a higher speed limit on the road, with suspension response sampled at each wheel.

**River.** One river enters at the north edge, drops over the waterfall, widens at the crossing and leaves at the south edge through a gorge. It is crossable only at the ford and the timber bridge, which makes the Crossing a natural gate. Shallow water slows the vehicle, spray and ripples respond to speed.

**Driving feel.** Heavy and grounded rather than arcade. Gravel slides a little, grass slows, water drags, and the High Pass has real danger from edges. The HUD already shows speed, pitch and roll, so keep slopes that make those numbers matter.

**Fast travel.** The map's Travel button teleports the player to a lit location with a short fade and an ambient line of text. Dark locations can still be reached by travel but arrive with a cold, empty start so the light feels earned.

**Off-road.** Allowed everywhere that is not too steep. The HUD already shows OFF ROAD, so reward it with small hidden finds rather than punishing it.

**Signposts.** Wooden arrow signs at each junction carry the location name and distance. They also guide the eye on the first drive.

## 6. The living world

The world should look different at every stage of play. Most of this is cheap: colour grading, particles and sound layers driven by one value, the number of signals lit.

**Light stages by signals lit**

| Signals lit | Sky | Ground and roads | Life |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Dark red-pink dusk, heavy cloud | Cold, almost black greens, dim roads | A few crows |
| 1 to 2 | Dusk lifts, a gold band on the horizon | First roads glow amber, one village lamp | Crows, first small birds |
| 3 to 4 | Clearer sky, first stars | Settlement windows lit, bridge glows | Flocks, fireflies near the river |
| 5 | Deep blue night with a warm horizon | Almost all roads lit, fire pits burning | Deer at the edges, owls |
| 6 | Clean starry night, then a slow sunrise | Whole ridge lit, beams between beacons | Everything active, a final flock overhead |

**Time of day.** The HUD clock already exists. Let it drift slowly (one full day in about 20 minutes) between dusk and night and let the visitor pause it in the Controls panel. Keep the dusk look as the default so screenshots always match the brand.

**Weather.** Mist in the Riverlands, ash drift in the Ashen Vale, wind streaks and blown dust on Fossil Ridge, low cloud at the High Pass. Weather is regional, so moving between regions feels like a change of place.

**Wildlife and ambient life**

- Crows and small birds (you already have a flock in the corner of the screen), with more species as light returns.
- Deer at forest edges, rabbits in the Lowlands, bats at dusk near the Old Giant.
- Fireflies and insects around water.
- Smoke from chimneys and cooking fires in the settlement.

**Props and set dressing.** Fence lines, haystacks, rusted cars, signposts, cairns, lanterns on posts, old cables strung between poles that end at the beacons (these read as the dead signal network and give the story its visual clue).

**Sound design**

- Region beds: wind in grass (Lowlands), water and birds (Riverlands), low hum and creak (Ashen Vale), thin high wind (Signal Range).
- Vehicle: engine, gravel, splash, wind at speed.
- Signal lighting: a rising tone and a soft fire crackle, different pitch per location so the six sounds form a short melody when all are lit.
- Music: sparse and slow, mostly pads and a single plucked instrument, silent while standing still.
- A master mute and a volume slider in Controls, with sound off by default until the first interaction.

## 7. Progression and secrets

Progress is one number, signals lit out of six, which your HUD already shows. Everything else reacts to it.

**Core loop.** Drive or walk to a location, read its content, press E to relight the signal, watch the world change, pick the next road. Each loop takes 1 to 2 minutes.

**Rewards for lighting a signal**

- A short camera move to the fire and a rising sound.
- The connecting roads and nearby windows light up.
- A field note unlocks: a two-line story fragment about the old signal keepers.
- A map update: fog clears around the location and along its roads.
- A small vehicle unlock at the Motor Works (a paint colour, a light colour or a horn) for each signal.

**Skip path.** A visitor in a hurry can use Skills Board, Work, Skills and Contact in the top bar at any time. The world is a reward, never a gate to the content.

**Optional rally.** A timed drive from the High Pass down to the Ashen Settlement with checkpoints. Best time is saved in the browser. It is optional and has no effect on the story.

**Hidden places and easter eggs**

- A cairn on Fossil Ridge that plays a hidden tone when you stop beside it.
- A tiny cabin behind the waterfall with a note and a link to a side project.
- A buried radio near the Watchtower ruin that plays a message when all six signals are lit.
- A signed rock at the quarry edge with your initials, A.S.
- A night-only light on the horizon that only appears after signal 6.
- A secret plank on the bridge that honks the horn.

**Ending.** Lighting the final signal triggers a beam from The Last Signal that reaches every beacon in turn. The camera pulls back to the whole lit valley and the Contact card appears with a clear call to action. Free roam continues afterwards.

**Returning visitors.** Progress is saved in the browser, so repeat visitors start with their lit signals, though the first minute plays quickly to save time.

## 8. Interface and map

The interface keeps your current look: dark glass panels, amber accents, a monospaced label font and a clean sans for titles.

**HUD (already built)**

- Top left: name and chapter title, signals lit counter.
- Top centre: clock. Top bar: Work, Skills, Contact, Back to Desktop, plus Skills Board, Open Map, Controls, Optional Rally, Motor Works.
- Right: pitch, roll and speed readout with road status.
- Bottom left: a field note line that changes with context.
- Bottom right: a context card for the nearby location with E and link actions.

**Map (upgraded in world-map.html)**

- Terrain with contours, forests, river, lake and a lit-window settlement.
- Pan, pinch and scroll zoom, a centre-on-me button and a fit-all button.
- Fog of war that clears as signals are lit and as the player travels.
- Layer chips for contours, roads, labels, landmarks and fog.
- Region names and landmarks that appear as you zoom in, a scale bar and a live coordinate and elevation readout.
- A location list with distance, signal state, keyboard stepping and one-click travel.

**Next map additions**

- Live player position and heading from the 3D world.
- A breadcrumb trail showing where the player has driven.
- Waypoint pinning to a location so a compass arrow appears in the HUD.
- A day and night tint that follows the world clock.
- A minimap in the HUD corner that reuses the same data.

**Controls.** Keep the panel short: drive keys, handbrake, camera, map (M), interact (E), reset vehicle, mute and time pause. Controls are shown on first load and then tucked behind the Controls button.

**Accessibility.** All content reachable without driving, reduced-motion option, keyboard-only map, readable contrast on every panel, and subtitles for any spoken or radio line.

## 9. Technical plan and build order

A world of this size runs in a browser if everything is built from instanced and low-poly pieces. These budgets are targets, not measurements, so test on a mid-range laptop and a phone early.

**Performance budget**

| Item | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Frame rate | 60 fps desktop, 30 fps mobile |
| Draw calls | Under 300 desktop, under 150 mobile |
| Triangles in view | Under 700k desktop, under 250k mobile |
| Terrain | One heightmap mesh in 4 to 9 chunks, 3 levels of detail |
| Trees and rocks | Instanced meshes, 3 variants each, culled by distance |
| Textures | 2k atlas per region, compressed, one shared normal map |
| Shadows | One directional light, one cascade, no real-time shadows on mobile |
| Initial download | Under 15 MB before first drive, the rest streamed |

Compact devices cap the camera range at 1.4 km to reduce mobile draw calls; desktop retains the full 8 km view. The final-signal pullback temporarily restores the full range to keep the valley panorama intact.

**Technique notes**

- Generate the terrain from the same height function used by the map so both always agree.
- Store roads as splines and build road meshes, posts and signs from them.
- Use the same grade-limited spline sample for the road mesh, terrain blend, bridge deck and vehicle surface.
- Fake distant detail with fog, billboards and baked lighting.
- Drive the light stage from one global value (signals lit) and tween colours rather than swapping scenes.
- Keep locations as data (id, name, position, content, lit) so the map, HUD, list and world all read from the same file.

**Asset checklist**

- Terrain heightmap and region splat map.
- Vehicle, workshop and customisation parts.
- Trees (pine, birch, burnt), rocks, grass cards, reeds.
- Signal fire, beacon tower, lanterns, posts and signs.
- Settlement houses (3 variants), bridge, hut, gate, cairns, fossil skeleton.
- Birds, deer, fireflies and particle effects.
- Audio: region beds, vehicle loops, six signal tones.

**Build order**

1. Lock the world data file: locations, roads, regions and landmarks, matching the new map.
2. Build the terrain and river from the height function, with the six location sites as flat plateaus.
3. Lay roads from splines and add signposts.
4. Build the Trailhead and the Ashen Settlement first, since they carry the most content.
5. Add the River Crossing, then the Old Giant and the High Pass.
6. Build The Last Signal and the ending sequence.
7. Add the light stages, weather and wildlife.
8. Add sound, secrets and the optional rally.
9. Connect the live player position to the map, then test on mobile and tune.
